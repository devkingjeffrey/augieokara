What I can tell you is in the work. I write across forms: folklore like Palm Kernel and The gods, contemporary comedy like Miss Broke Lynn, the satirical fable How To Make Money As An African Man, and non-fiction like To Create Is Divine.

My stories are rooted in African, and especially Igbo, life: village deities and dreaded priests, campus hustles, fathers passing down hard wisdom. The names, the humour and the texture all come from that world.

One idea ties it together. In To Create Is Divine I make the case that making things is close to sacred. To consume is human; to create is Divine. That is why I write.
