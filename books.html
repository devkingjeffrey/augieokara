Palm Kernel and The gods

  • Igbo folklore
  • Tradition
  • Power

Obiofuru lives in the shadow of Ngele, a deity known for swift and fearsome punishment, served by the dreaded Dibia priest Mazi Kamma. When two restless schoolboys strip a farm of its fallen palm kernels, a small act of mischief sets tradition, authority and the gods on a collision course. Told with Igbo texture and dark humour, it is a village tale about fear, power and consequence.

Miss Broke Lynn

  • Contemporary
  • Humour
  • Money

Everyone calls her Lynn, and Lynn never has money. Told almost entirely in fast, funny dialogue, this contemporary Nigerian story follows a squatting university student through roommate showdowns, a looming move-out date and the five people she might squeeze 500k from. A sharp, affectionate look at money, pride and friendship among young people getting by.

How To Make Money As An African Man

  • Satire
  • Fable
  • Fatherhood

Akurukpu was raised an orphan and vowed his sons would learn everything he never did. One evening he asks them the most important thing in life. Family, says one. Knowledge, says another. Love, says the third. All wrong, he tells them: money rules above them all. A satirical fable, subtitled Don't Apply, about fatherhood, values and the hard lessons one generation hands the next.

To Create Is Divine

  • Creativity
  • The information age
  • Non-fiction

After God, Augie Okpara argues, the next creators are writers: people who make something out of nothing but thought and words. To Create Is Divine is a brisk, personal manifesto for the information age. It asks why you create, what kind of creator you are, and how to stop being only an audience for everyone else's content. Its closing line sets the tone of all her work: to consume is human, to create is Divine.
